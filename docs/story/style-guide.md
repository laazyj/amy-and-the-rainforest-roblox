# Style guide — writing in Clara's voice

How to write new Lines, Objectives, Journal entries and story proposals
so they sound like Clara's book. Everything here is drawn from her text in
[`canon.md`](canon.md). Terms are as defined in the
[glossary](../GLOSSARY.md).

New Lines in Clara's voice are **not canon** until she says so. They are
proposed in a PR (or in `docs/story/proposals/` for a new Chapter), marked
"new Lines, not canon, for approval" in the PR description, and approved
by the owner and Clara.

## 1. What her voice sounds like

**Short, plain sentences that move.** Most of her sentences are 5 to 16
words (median 9): "She tryed again but Sam brang her back." "It was a
bautyfull paradise!" Each one says one thing that happens. There is no
description for its own sake; the forest is just "a dark forest" that
"loomed".

**Long sentences only for a chain of events.** When things tumble
forward, she joins them with "and", "who" and "but" instead of starting
new sentences: "She rushed Home and told her mum who told her dad who told
the village." Use this for a rush of consequences, at most once in a scene.

**Amy does; the story tells.** The Narrator reports what Amy did, in the
past tense, in the third person: "Amy managed to get outside." Feelings
show through actions and exclamation marks, not through naming them:
"Amy discoverd so many animals!" rather than "Amy felt amazed".

**Warm and on the side of the forest.** Nobody in the story is a villain.
The village is frightened ("they thought it was a thret to humankind"),
Dad is protective, Sam is doing his job. Problems are solved by Amy
explaining, and grown-ups listening: "They took her seriously and stoped."

**Humour comes from situations, not jokes.** Sam bringing Amy back every
single time is funny because it keeps happening. The game Lines add
playful asides in Amy's voice ("You are the best guard dog in the whole
world, Sam, and it is EXTREMELY annoying."); the Narrator stays plain.

**Exclamation marks for wonder.** "As soon as she went in Amy saw it was a
world parallel to thier own!" Use them for discovery, not for every Line.

**Simple, concrete words.** Colours and animals are named directly:
"a maroon squirrel, an orch fox and a golden lion". Prefer the word a
seven-year-old would use.

## 2. Spelling

Clara's spellings in canon are kept **on purpose**. They are part of the
book's charm and are locked with the canon Lines:

| Clara writes | Standard |
|---|---|
| beautyfull, bautyfull | beautiful |
| sunday | Sunday |
| it to Dangerous | it's too dangerous |
| tryed | tried |
| out side | outside |
| thier | their |
| brang | brought |
| infront | in front |
| Explore (capital) | explore |
| discoverd | discovered |
| wos | was |
| orch | orange |
| Home (capital) | home |
| thret | threat |
| Lukily | Luckily |
| enugh | enough |
| stoped | stopped |

**The rule for new text:** use standard British English spelling (the
game already says "colour", "Mum", "favourite") unless Clara herself wrote
the words. Do not imitate her spellings in new Lines: it reads as mocking
her rather than honouring her, and it blurs which words are hers.
("vilage" is no longer kept: see [`canon.md`](canon.md#decisions).)

## 3. Reading level: ages 7 to 12

- **One idea per Line**, and at most two short sentences. A Line must fit
  the dialogue box without scrolling on a phone (see
  [`design/feel.md`](../design/feel.md)); as a guide, 25 words or about
  140 characters at most, 15 words or fewer for most Lines.
- **Everyday words.** If a younger reader might not know a word, use a
  simpler one or make the meaning obvious from the Line itself.
- **No timed reading.** Nothing the player must read disappears on a
  timer; Lines advance when the player chooses.
- **Objectives are instructions**, in standard spelling, starting with a
  verb, 10 words or fewer where possible: "Ask Dad if you can go outside".
- **Capitals for one shouted word**, as the game Lines do ("He was ALWAYS
  watching."), at most one per Line.

## 4. Off limits

Unless Clara says otherwise, new text must never:

- **change the ending**: Amy stops the machine by staying in front of it
  and explaining; the village takes her seriously and stops; the forest is
  saved and cared for;
- **alter characters' personalities**: Amy is curious, brave and
  determined; Dad is protective; Mum is the one Amy rushes to tell; Sam is
  loyal and always watching; the forest animals are friendly, and the
  lion is gentle;
- include anything outside an **all-ages rating**: no violence beyond the
  threat of the machine to the trees, no injury, nothing frightening for
  its own sake, no romance, no unkindness played for laughs;
- mention or invite **purchases**, Robux, or anything for sale;
- contain **external links**, web addresses, or references to sites
  outside the game;
- use anyone's **full name**: the author is "Clara" only, in credits and
  everywhere else; real people other than the family roles in the story
  are never named;
- change a canon Line (see [the rule](canon.md#the-rule)).

New story facts (new Characters, places, events) come only through an
approved proposal in `docs/story/proposals/`, never inside a Line written
for another purpose.

## 5. Examples

These examples show voice only. They are not story and are not proposed
for the game.

### Lines that fit

1. **Narrator:** "Sam waited by the gate all afternoon."
   Short, plain, past tense, one thing happening; Sam is still the loyal
   dog who is always watching.
2. **Amy:** "Look at all the colours! I'm going to remember every single
   one."
   Wonder through an exclamation, everyday words, true to Amy's
   curiosity, standard spelling.
3. **Narrator:** "Amy told Mum, and Mum told Dad, and Dad told the whole
   village."
   A chain of events joined by "and", in the rhythm of her "who told her
   dad who told the village", in plain words.

### Lines that do not fit

1. **Narrator:** "Amy wos so happy she tryed to hug the fox."
   Imitates Clara's spellings in new text. New Lines use standard
   spelling.
2. **Narrator:** "The verdant canopy shimmered with an iridescent,
   almost otherworldly luminescence that defied comprehension."
   Too long, too abstract, words a seven-year-old would not know, and
   description for its own sake.
3. **Golden Lion:** "Get out of my forest before I eat you!"
   Changes the lion's personality (he is gentle) and adds threat outside
   the story's warm, all-ages tone.
